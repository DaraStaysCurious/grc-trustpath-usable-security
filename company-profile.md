# TrustPath — Company Profile

## What TrustPath Does

TrustPath is a B2B SaaS platform that helps security and procurement teams assess, score, and continuously monitor the risk posture of their third-party vendors. Customers use TrustPath to send vendor security questionnaires, collect and score evidence (SOC 2 reports, penetration test summaries, insurance certificates), track remediation items, and maintain an ongoing risk register across their vendor portfolio.

## Who Uses It

- **Security analysts** — review vendor questionnaire responses and supporting evidence, flag risk items, and approve or escalate vendors
- **Procurement teams** — initiate vendor assessments as part of the sourcing/onboarding process, often under contractual or budget-cycle deadlines
- **GRC/compliance staff** — maintain the vendor risk register for audit purposes and report on third-party risk posture to leadership

## Operating Environment

TrustPath customers are mid-size to enterprise companies with a formal third-party risk management program, typically required by their own SOC 2 or ISO 27001 obligations to demonstrate vendor oversight. Assessment volume is uneven — vendor onboarding tends to cluster around contract renewal cycles, new product launches, and fiscal year-end procurement pushes, creating periods where a small security/procurement team must clear a large backlog of vendor evaluations against a fixed deadline.

## System Scope (for this assessment)

- Vendor questionnaire builder and distribution
- Evidence upload and review workspace
- Automated risk scoring engine
- Remediation tracking and vendor risk register
- Role-based access (security analyst, procurement, admin)

## Constraints Driving This Assessment

- Assessment throughput is measured and reported internally — analysts are evaluated in part on how quickly they clear the vendor queue
- Deadlines are frequently externally imposed (a vendor contract signing date, a compliance audit window) rather than set by the security team itself
- The platform's own controls must hold up under the same time pressure its users are managing on behalf of their vendors — a irony this assessment treats as central, not incidental
